This leaflet explains patient-initiated follow-up, usually called PIFU. It tells you what it means, how to arrange an appointment if you need one, and when to get urgent help instead. Please ask your eye team about anything that is not clear.

## What is PIFU?

PIFU means that, instead of coming back for check-ups at set times, you (or your carer) arrange an appointment yourself when you need one, for example if your symptoms change or your condition flares up [1, 2].

Your eye team is still looking after you. You simply contact us when you need us, rather than coming in when you are well [1, 3].

## Why do we offer it?

- If your condition is stable, a routine check-up often does not change your treatment, but still means time off work, travel and arrangements [2].
- With PIFU, you can be seen when you actually need help, for example during a flare-up [2].
- Freeing up routine appointments means people who need to be seen urgently, or whose sight is at risk, can be seen sooner [1].

## Is PIFU right for me?

PIFU is for people whose condition is stable or low risk, and who can keep an eye on their own symptoms [1]. It is a decision you make together with your clinician [1].

{{site.pifu.conditions}}

**It is your choice.** If you would rather keep having regular appointments, tell us [1, 2]. If you start PIFU and change your mind, tell us and we will go back to booking regular appointments for you [1, 2].

If you find it hard to contact us yourself, a carer or family member can arrange appointments for you [1, 4].

## How long will I be on PIFU?

You will usually be on PIFU for {{site.pifu.duration}}, unless your clinician agrees a different time with you. If you contact us during that time, we may extend it [4].

## When should I use PIFU?

Contact us if your condition flares up, if your symptoms change, or if you have a problem with your treatment or eye drops [1]. Your clinic letter will tell you which symptoms to look out for [1].

**PIFU is only for the eye condition we have been treating you for.** For anything else, contact your GP, optometrist or NHS 111 [1, 5]. If you are under more than one clinic, PIFU only applies to the clinic that offered it [6].

## How do I arrange an appointment?

Call {{site.pifu.phone}}.

{{site.pifu.how_to_contact}}

When you call, say that you are on PIFU with the eye clinic, and give your name, date of birth and hospital or NHS number. Your NHS number is at the top of your hospital letters [5, 7].

The person who answers the phone cannot give medical advice [8]. A member of the clinical team will look at your concerns and decide whether you need advice or an appointment [2]. {{site.pifu.response}} This may be a phone, video or face-to-face appointment [5].

## PIFU is not for emergencies

**If you have a sudden problem with your eye, do not wait for a PIFU appointment** [2, 6]. For example, get urgent help if you have [13]:

- a sudden change in your vision
- a red eye and it hurts to look at light
- a red eye and you wear contact lenses
- an injury to your eye

Call {{site.contacts.urgent_phone}} if you need urgent help or advice.

## What happens at the end of PIFU?

If you have not needed to contact us by the end of your PIFU time, we will discharge you and let you and your GP know [4, 9]. You can still see your optometrist (optician) as usual. If you have problems later, your GP or optometrist can refer you back to us [1, 9].

Please tell us if your address or phone number changes while you are on PIFU [10].

## Does PIFU work?

Research in other long-term conditions has found that patient-initiated appointments made little or no difference to relapses, anxiety or how satisfied people were, compared with routine appointments, although the evidence is limited [11]. Its wider effects on patients and services are still uncertain [12].
