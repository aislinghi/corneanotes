This leaflet is for people with blepharitis. It explains what it is, how to look after your eyelids, and what other treatments can help. Please ask your eye team about anything that is not clear.

## What is blepharitis?

Blepharitis is long-lasting inflammation of the edges of the eyelids. It is common [1]. There are two main types, which can happen together [1]:

- **anterior blepharitis,** affecting the front edge of the eyelids, where the eyelashes grow
- **posterior blepharitis,** affecting the inside edges of the eyelids, where small oil glands (meibomian glands) are found. This is usually caused by these glands not working properly [1].

Blepharitis cannot be passed on to other people [2].

## What are the symptoms?

Symptoms often come and go [2]. They include:

- sore, itchy eyelids and eyes [2]
- a gritty feeling in the eyes [2]
- flakes or crusts at the roots of the eyelashes [2]
- eyelids sticking together in the morning [2]
- red eyelid edges, dry or burning eyes, and sometimes blurred vision [3]

## What causes it?

It is usually linked to bacteria that normally live on the skin [1, 2]. It can also be caused by tiny mites (Demodex) that live in the eyelid glands [1], by skin conditions such as seborrhoeic dermatitis [2], or by the eyelid oil glands not working properly [2]. It is more common in people with skin conditions such as eczema and acne [3].

## Can it be cured?

There is no cure, but the symptoms can be controlled [3]. Most people need to keep looking after their eyelids in the long term, even when their symptoms have settled [1, 2, 4].

## Cleaning your eyelids (lid hygiene)

Cleaning your eyelids every day is the main treatment for both types of blepharitis [4, 5]. It can ease symptoms [6]. Do this twice a day [2], with clean hands. Take out contact lenses first [3].

1. **Warm compress.** Soak a clean flannel or cotton wool in warm (not hot) water and hold it on your closed eyelids for 5 to 10 minutes [2]. Take care not to burn your skin [3].
2. **Massage.** Gently massage your eyelids for about 30 seconds [2], rolling your finger towards the lashes to help clear the oil glands [3].
3. **Clean.** Gently wipe along the edges of your eyelids with cotton wool or a cotton bud to remove flakes and crusts. A small amount of baby shampoo in water may help [2].

Keep cleaning your eyelids even when your symptoms have cleared up [2].

## While you have symptoms

- Do not wear contact lenses [2].
- Do not wear eye make-up, especially eyeliner and mascara [2].

## Other treatments

- **Lubricating eye drops** can help with dryness [3].
- **Antibiotic cream or ointment** for the eyelids may be suggested if cleaning your eyelids has not helped [2]. It can ease symptoms in anterior blepharitis [6].
- **Antibiotic tablets** taken for a few months are sometimes used for posterior blepharitis [4]. The evidence for them is limited, and they can cause side effects [7]. Some, such as doxycycline, are not usually used in pregnancy or in children under 12 [8].
- **Tea tree oil** products are sometimes used for blepharitis caused by mites. It is uncertain how well they work, and lower strengths may cause less irritation [9].
- **Omega-3 (fish oil) supplements** did not help dry eye symptoms more than a dummy pill in a large trial [10].
- **Intense pulsed light (IPL),** a light treatment for the eyelid oil glands, has been studied, but it is uncertain whether it helps [11].

## Possible complications

- **A chalazion,** a lump in the eyelid caused by a blocked oil gland, which can become red and sore if infected [3].
- **Dry eye** [1].
- **Changes to the cornea,** the clear window at the front of the eye, which happen less often [3].

## When to get help

See your GP or optician if your symptoms do not improve, or get worse [2].

Get urgent help if you have eye pain, changes in your vision, or very red eyes [2], or spreading redness with a lot of eyelid swelling [3].
