This leaflet is for people who have a scratch on the front of the eye, called a corneal abrasion. It explains what it is, how it is treated and when to get help. Please ask your eye team about anything that is not clear.

## What is a corneal abrasion?

A corneal abrasion is a scratch on the cornea, the clear window at the front of your eye [1]. It is common [2]. It is usually caused by a small accident, such as a fingernail, a twig, paper or a mascara brush, by a contact lens, or by a speck of grit under the eyelid [2, 3].

## What are the symptoms?

A corneal abrasion can be very painful, because the cornea is one of the most sensitive parts of the body [2]. Your eye may also be red, watery and sensitive to light, your eyelids may be swollen, and your vision may be blurred [2, 3].

## Go to A&E straight away if

- something has hit your eye at high speed, for example while using power tools or mowing the lawn
- a sharp object has pierced your eye
- your sight has changed since the injury
- blood or pus is coming from your eye [4]

Do not drive yourself. Ask someone to take you, or call 999 [4].

## How is it treated?

Your eye will be examined, usually with a drop of orange dye (fluorescein) that makes the scratch easier to see [2].

- **Eye drops or ointment.** Antibiotic or anti-inflammatory drops are often recommended, depending on the type and size of the scratch [2]. Antibiotics are given to reduce the chance of infection, although research has not shown clearly how much they help [3, 5].
- **Lubricants.** Artificial tear drops or ointment can help keep your eye moist while it heals [3].
- **Painkillers.** Paracetamol or ibuprofen can help ease the pain [4].
- **Dilating drops** are sometimes given to relieve discomfort [2]. They blur your vision for a while, so do not drive if you have had them [6].
- **Numbing (anaesthetic) drops** are only used to examine your eye. They are not given to take home, because using them repeatedly slows healing and can damage the eye [2, 3, 6].
- **An eye patch** is not usually needed. Research has found that patching a simple scratch does not help it heal or reduce pain [7].

## How long will it take to heal?

Most scratches heal within one to two days, and the pain settles as the eye heals [3, 8]. Your eye may feel gritty for a few weeks [3].

## While your eye heals

- Try not to rub or touch your eye [3].
- Do not wear eye make-up until your eye is better [4].
- **Contact lenses:** do not wear them until your eye is better [4]. {{site.aftercare.abrasion.contact_lenses}}

## Can it come back?

Some people get further episodes of pain weeks or months later, often on waking, even after the scratch has healed [3, 6]. This is called recurrent corneal erosion. In a UK study, about {{risk.cornea.abrasion.recurrent_symptoms_3m}} people had mild symptoms come back in the three months after a scratch, but serious recurrent erosion was not common [9]. If this happens, contact us. There is a separate leaflet about recurrent corneal erosion.

## When to get help

Contact us, or get help from NHS 111, if:

- your eye is not getting better after 24 hours [4]
- your vision becomes more blurred, or the pain or redness gets a lot worse [3]

## Preventing eye injuries

Wear protective glasses or goggles when working with metal, wood or machines, when handling hazardous chemicals, and when playing contact sports [8].
