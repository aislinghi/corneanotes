This leaflet is for people with marginal keratitis. It explains what it is, how it is treated, and how to make it less likely to come back. Please ask your eye team about anything that is not clear.

## What is marginal keratitis?

Marginal keratitis is inflammation at the edge of the cornea, the clear window at the front of the eye [1, 2]. It is thought to be a reaction to bacteria that normally live on the skin near the edges of the eyelids [1, 2]. It is an inflammation, not an infection [1].

It is usually linked to blepharitis, a common condition affecting the edges of the eyelids [2, 3]. Less often, it is linked to wearing contact lenses [2].

## What are the symptoms?

- a red, painful eye [1, 2]
- watering [1, 3]
- sensitivity to light [4]
- a feeling of something in the eye [3]
- small white spots or ulcers at the edge of the cornea [2, 3]

It often comes back [1].

## Contact lens wearers

Marginal keratitis can look like a corneal infection, which is more serious [1]. If you wear contact lenses and have a painful red eye, take your lenses out and get your eye checked urgently [3, 5]. An eye specialist can usually tell the two apart when they examine your eye: in contact lens wearers, inflammation without infection tends to cause smaller spots and less pain than an infection [6].

## How is it treated?

Marginal keratitis usually gets better on its own, but treatment can help it settle more quickly [1, 4].

- **Steroid eye drops** reduce the inflammation, often together with antibiotic drops or ointment [1, 2, 4]. Steroid drops can make infection more likely, so your eye team will check your eye, including the pressure inside it, while you use them [4]. Only use steroid drops as prescribed.
- **Treating the blepharitis** is also important [1, 3].
- **Sunglasses** can help with light sensitivity [1, 4].

There is limited evidence from clinical trials about how well these treatments work [4].

## Stopping it coming back

Marginal keratitis often comes back if the blepharitis is not treated [3]. Clean your eyelids regularly, even when you have no symptoms [2]. There is a separate leaflet about blepharitis, which explains how to do this.

## When to get help urgently

Contact us straight away if your eye becomes more red or painful, your vision becomes more blurred, or the white spots on the cornea get bigger [2, 3]. If you wear contact lenses and have a red, painful eye, you could have an infection, so get help urgently [5].
