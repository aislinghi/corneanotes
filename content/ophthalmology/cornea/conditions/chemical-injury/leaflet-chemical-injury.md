This leaflet is for people who have had a chemical in their eye, and their families. It explains what to do straight away, what happens in hospital, and what to expect. Please ask your eye team about anything that is not clear.

## First aid: what to do straight away

If a strong chemical, such as oven cleaner or bleach, gets in your eye [1]:

1. **Rinse your eye with water immediately.** Use clean, cool water from a tap or shower, or bottled water if you are not at home [1].
2. **Hold your eye open** and run lots of water over it for **at least 20 minutes**. The flow should not be too strong [1].
3. **Go to A&E or call 999,** and keep rinsing while you wait for help. Do not drive yourself [1].
4. **Take the container with you,** as the information on it can help with treatment [1].
5. Do not rub your eye [1].

Rinsing straight away is the most important thing you can do to protect your sight [2, 3].

## Why chemical injuries are serious

Chemicals can damage the surface of the eye and, in severe cases, the inside of the eye [2, 3]. Alkalis, such as ammonia in some household cleaners, drain and oven cleaners, and cement and plaster, usually cause the most damage, because they pass easily into the eye [2, 3]. Chemical injuries can happen at work, during DIY, at home, or in an assault [2].

## What happens in hospital

- Your eye will be numbed with drops and rinsed again, often for a long time [4, 5].
- The acidity of your eye (its pH) will be tested with a strip of paper, and rinsing will continue until it is back to normal [4, 5].
- Any particles of the chemical will be removed from under the eyelids [4].
- An eye specialist will examine your eye on the same day, and may admit you to hospital if the injury is serious [2].

## Treatment

Treatment aims to calm inflammation, help the surface of the eye heal, and prevent infection and other problems [3]. Your eye pressure will also be checked [4]. Your eye team will choose the eye drops and any tablets you need.

For more serious burns, a thin layer of tissue called amniotic membrane may be placed over the eye to help it heal. There is some evidence it may help moderate burns, but it has not been shown to help the most severe burns [6]. There is a separate leaflet about amniotic membrane.

## What is the outlook?

It depends on how severe the injury is. Mild and moderate injuries usually heal well with eye drops [7]. Severe injuries can cause lasting damage:

- In a UK study of severe chemical injuries, about {{risk.cornea.chemical_injury.severe_ongoing_problems}} people still needed treatment six months later, and about {{risk.cornea.chemical_injury.severe_surgery}} needed surgery [8].
- In a London study of severe injuries, about {{risk.cornea.chemical_injury.severe_vision_below_6_12}} people had vision worse than 6/12 [3].
- The most severe injuries can destroy the stem cells at the edge of the cornea that keep its surface healthy [7]. Later, these can sometimes be replaced with a stem cell transplant from the other eye. This was successful in about {{risk.cornea.chemical_injury.stem_cell_graft_success}} eyes in published studies [9].

Other problems after severe injuries include dry eye, scarring of the cornea and cataract [3].

**Please keep all your follow-up appointments.** In one study, many people with severe injuries missed them [3].

## Preventing chemical injuries

- Wear eye protection, such as safety glasses or goggles, when working with chemicals, cement or plaster [10, 11].
- Keep cleaning products, laundry capsules and dishwasher tablets out of children's reach. Laundry capsules and dishwasher tablets can cause chemical burns if the liquid gets into a child's eyes [12].

## When to get help

Contact us straight away if your eye becomes more painful, your vision gets worse, or there is pus or blood coming from your eye [1].
