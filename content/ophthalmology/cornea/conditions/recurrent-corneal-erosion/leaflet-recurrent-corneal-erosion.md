This leaflet is for people with recurrent corneal erosion. It explains what it is, what you can do to help, and the treatments available if it keeps coming back. Please ask your eye team about anything that is not clear.

## What is recurrent corneal erosion?

In recurrent corneal erosion, the surface layer of the cornea, the clear window at the front of the eye, does not stick down properly and keeps breaking down [1]. While you sleep, the weak surface can stick to the inside of your eyelid. When you open your eye, it can tear away again [2].

## What are the symptoms?

- sudden sharp pain, typically when you wake up, or sometimes waking you in the night [1]
- watering [1]
- sensitivity to light and blurred vision [1, 2]

Attacks can keep coming back over weeks or months [1], and can start many months or even years after an injury [2].

## What causes it?

It often starts after a minor scratch to the eye, most commonly from a fingernail [1, 3]. It can also happen in people with a condition called a corneal dystrophy, in which the surface of the cornea is unusually delicate [1].

## What can I do?

The main treatment is to reduce the friction between your eye and your eyelid, using lubricants [1]:

- **during the day:** lubricating eye drops or gel
- **at night:** a lubricating eye ointment before you go to sleep [4]

Keep using them for at least three months after your last attack, even if your eye feels fine [4]. A salty (hypertonic saline) ointment at bedtime is an alternative, and in a UK trial it worked as well as a plain lubricating ointment [4, 5].

If you have an attack, your eye team may also give you drops to ease the pain and antibiotic ointment while the surface heals [4].

## If it keeps coming back

- **A course of tablets.** If symptoms continue despite lubricants, a three-month course of a tablet from the tetracycline family may be considered [4]. The evidence for this is unclear [6].
- **A bandage contact lens** can be used for a while, although studies have not shown a clear advantage over lubricants alone [4].
- **A procedure** may be offered if other treatments have not worked [4]. There are several options, but research has not yet shown clearly which is best [4, 6]:
  - **Removing the loose surface,** sometimes with gentle polishing of the layer beneath (diamond burr polishing). In a trial, adding polishing led to fewer attacks than removing the surface alone [7].
  - **Alcohol delamination.** After numbing drops, a weak alcohol solution is placed on the eye for about 40 seconds and the loose surface layer is peeled off. A bandage contact lens is then put in while the eye heals [8]. In a UK study, about {{risk.cornea.recurrent_erosion.alcohol_success}} eyes were successfully treated [9]. In another UK study, erosions came back within three years in about {{risk.cornea.recurrent_erosion.alcohol_recurrence_3y}} eyes [10].
  - **PTK laser treatment** (phototherapeutic keratectomy). After numbing drops, the surface layer is removed and a laser smooths the layer beneath, leaving a smooth surface for new surface cells to grow over [11]. A bandage contact lens may be put in at the end [12]. NICE has found the evidence good enough for it to be used routinely [13].
  - In a trial comparing alcohol delamination with PTK, symptoms fully or partly settled in about {{risk.cornea.recurrent_erosion.alcohol_vs_ptk}} eyes with either treatment [14].

## What are the risks of PTK?

Across published studies [15]:

- erosions came back in about {{risk.cornea.recurrent_erosion.ptk_recurrence}} eyes
- about {{risk.cornea.recurrent_erosion.ptk_haze}} eyes had some haze in the cornea, which usually cleared with time
- about {{risk.cornea.recurrent_erosion.ptk_long_sight}} eyes became a little more long-sighted, so your glasses prescription may change
- about {{risk.cornea.recurrent_erosion.ptk_vision_loss}} eyes lost a little sharpness of vision, usually one line on the sight chart

## What is the outlook?

- Most people improve with simple measures. In a UK trial, only about {{risk.cornea.recurrent_erosion.needed_more_than_ointment}} people using nightly ointment needed further treatment [5].
- Attacks can carry on for a long time, but are usually mild. In a UK study, about {{risk.cornea.recurrent_erosion.symptoms_4y}} people still had occasional attacks four years later, typically about every two months, with mild pain [16].
- Attacks were more likely to carry on in people with a corneal dystrophy (about {{risk.cornea.recurrent_erosion.symptoms_4y_ebmd}}) than in people whose erosions started after an injury (about {{risk.cornea.recurrent_erosion.symptoms_4y_injury}}) [16].

## When to get help

A breakdown of the surface makes the cornea more likely to become infected [6]. Contact us if your eye becomes more red or painful, or your vision becomes more blurred [2].
