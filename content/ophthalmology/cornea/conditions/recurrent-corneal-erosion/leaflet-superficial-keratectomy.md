This leaflet is for people who have been offered a superficial keratectomy. It explains what the procedure is, why it is done, what to expect afterwards, and the possible risks. Please ask your eye team about anything that is not clear.

## What is a superficial keratectomy?

The cornea is the clear window at the front of the eye. A superficial keratectomy removes its surface layer, and any rough or abnormal tissue just beneath it, while leaving the deeper layers untouched [1, 2]. A new, smooth surface then grows back.

Sometimes a weak alcohol solution is placed on the eye first, to loosen the surface cells so they are easier to remove. This is called alcohol delamination [1, 3]. The layer beneath may also be gently polished (diamond burr polishing) [2].

## Why is it done?

It is used when the surface of the cornea has become rough or uneven [1, 4], for example because of:

- **recurrent corneal erosion,** when the surface keeps breaking down and causing painful attacks [1, 4]
- **epithelial basement membrane dystrophy (EBMD),** which can cause erosions or blurred vision [2, 4]
- **Salzmann nodules** (see below) [2, 4]
- **scarring** on the surface of the cornea [1, 2]

There are separate leaflets on recurrent corneal erosion and EBMD.

## Salzmann nodules

Salzmann nodular degeneration causes raised, grey-white or bluish lumps (nodules) on the cornea. It is more common in women and often affects both eyes [6]. It is linked to other conditions of the eye surface, and to previous eye surgery [5].

- Nodules may cause no symptoms, or a feeling of something in the eye, or blurred vision [5].
- Most people can be treated with lubricating drops, warm compresses and eyelid cleaning [5].
- Only some people need surgery, between about {{risk.cornea.superficial_keratectomy.salzmann_surgery_needed}} people in published studies [5].

## Before cataract surgery

Before cataract surgery, the eye is measured to choose the strength of the lens implant. An uneven cornea, for example from EBMD or Salzmann nodules, can make these measurements less accurate [7]. Treating the surface first can give more reliable measurements [7]. The measurements are then repeated once the surface has settled. In one study, this was done at least a month later [7].

## What happens on the day?

- Numbing eye drops are used, so you are awake [1, 3].
- Your eye and eyelids are cleaned and a sterile cover is placed over your face [1].
- It should not be painful. You will see a bright light and feel fluid on your eye [1].
- It usually takes about 15 to 30 minutes [1, 3].
- At the end, a soft "bandage" contact lens is usually placed on the eye, or the eye may be padded closed [1, 3].

## Afterwards

- **Pain.** Your eye will feel as if it has been scratched, because the surface has been removed. It is often painful for the first few days, and will water a lot [1]. You may be given numbing drops to use only in the first 12 hours if the pain is severe. Do not use them for longer, because they slow healing [1]. Painkillers may help a little [1].
- **Healing.** The surface usually heals within about 3 to 7 days, and vision improves over the following week or two [3].
- **Drops.** You will use antibiotic drops for about two weeks, and sometimes steroid drops too [1, 9].
- **Avoid** rubbing your eye, swimming, eye make-up and dusty places for at least two weeks [3].
- **Benefits.** A smoother surface usually shows within two to four weeks. If the aim is to stop erosions, it may take some months to be sure it has worked [1].
- **Driving.** You must be able to read a number plate at 20 metres. You may meet this a few days after surgery, but ask at your follow-up appointment if you are unsure [1].

## What are the risks?

- **Infection** of the cornea while the surface heals [1, 3].
- **Slow healing** of the surface [1, 3].
- **Haze or scarring.** A faint haze in the cornea is the most common problem. It is usually mild and often fades [2, 8]. In one study of people with EBMD, about {{risk.cornea.ebmd.debridement_haze}} eyes developed some haze [8].
- **The problem coming back.** Erosions or roughness can return [1]. In the same EBMD study, the chance of EBMD coming back within five years was about {{risk.cornea.ebmd.debridement_recurrence_5y}} [8]. In a US study of Salzmann nodules, the nodules came back in about {{risk.cornea.superficial_keratectomy.salzmann_recurrence}} eyes after surgery [6].
- **A change in your glasses prescription,** especially after removing Salzmann nodules, because the shape of the cornea changes [9].

## When to get help urgently

Contact us straight away if [3]:

- your pain gets worse, or has not started to improve after the first two days
- your vision suddenly gets worse
- your eye becomes more red or swollen, or you notice yellow or green discharge
